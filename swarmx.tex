\begin{abstract}
Agent-assisted research fragments state across conversations, computations, artifacts, team actions,
and reusable knowledge. SwarmX is open-source orchestration software that connects these records while
preserving their authoritative owners. Runtime adapters retain native histories; Science manages
revisioned research entities and content-addressed artifacts; Swarm coordinates durable tasks, effect
recovery, and independent verification; and a shared semantic memory store retains research knowledge under
explicit revision and approval controls. Two executed client workflows publish an RO-Crate science
record and a verified four-member knowledge-admission result. Executable fixtures, integration
tests, and a bounded transition model exercise artifact integrity, runtime boundaries, and six
coordination invariants. SwarmX provides an inspectable route from agent execution to portable
scientific records and bounded concurrent coordination.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and significance}
\label{sec:motivation}

LLM agents can search literature, run code, edit files, invoke instruments and services, delegate
work, and draft manuscripts. A single research result may consequently depend on a native
conversation history, several notebook or command executions, mutable workspace files, an
experiment record, review decisions, and knowledge carried into later sessions. These records have
different owners and lifecycles. A transcript records interaction but does not establish which
artifact version entered a claim; a file tree retains outputs but not the decision that admitted
them; and a workflow log does not by itself preserve the research question or later knowledge use.
The resulting fragmentation makes agent-assisted research difficult to inspect, resume, and
transfer.

Recent SoftwareX publications demonstrate usable LLM software for domain assistance, validated
function invocation, model-bias testing, and heterogeneous-model routing
~\cite{do2025,aldana-martin2025,morales2025,cheikhtourad2026}. End-to-end systems now span
complementary parts of discovery. The peer-reviewed AI Scientist automates computational
machine-learning research from ideation through manuscript review; Co-Scientist uses an
asynchronous multi-agent process to generate and refine biomedical hypotheses; and the Virtual Lab
combines an AI principal investigator and specialist agents with human feedback and wet-laboratory
validation~\cite{lu2026,gottweis2026coscientist,swanson2025virtuallab}. ScienceAgentBench instead
evaluates 102 executable tasks drawn from 44 peer-reviewed papers and shows that current agents
solve only a minority, even when expert knowledge is supplied~\cite{chen2025scienceagentbench}.
SwarmX addresses the infrastructure below these research policies: durable ownership,
coordination, and linkage of the records produced by native agent runtimes and scientific tools.

Computational-provenance systems supply essential parts of this infrastructure. Whole Tale captures
reproducible environments, AiiDA manages computational workflows, noWorkflow captures Python
execution, and ProvBook enriches notebooks~\cite{brinckman2019,pizzi2016,pimentel2017,samuel2018}.
Workflow Run RO-Crate packages workflow executions, and PROV-AGENT represents agent interactions
~\cite{leo2024,souza2025a}. The FAIR principles and RO-Crate provide the exchange layer for
discoverable, reusable research objects~\cite{wilkinson2016,soiland-reyes2022}. SwarmX composes
these ideas around a cross-owner operational record rather than a new universal store.

Contemporary agent systems increasingly make claims and evidence first-class. ResearchLoop records
questions, evidence, claim ledgers, and closeouts in a repository-backed control plane; LEDGER
builds claim-to-evidence trace graphs over agent sessions; artifact-centered observability connects
scientific claims, artifacts, evaluations, and lineage; and PaperTrail presents claim-level
provenance for scholarly review~\cite{xia2026,kim2026,yin2026,martin-boyle2026}. Table~\ref{tab:related}
locates SwarmX within this landscape. Its software boundary combines research records with durable
multi-agent task and effect control, then carries independently verified outcomes into owner-held
knowledge.

SwarmX differs from these systems in two concrete ways. First, it does not maintain a claim ledger
of its own: claim nodes are first-class Science entities backed by the append-only Journal, and an
evidence relation is a typed reference from a claim to a run, dataset, or figure. Second, the
claim-to-evidence path is portable: Science exports the project graph as RO-Crate 1.3, so a
ResearchLoop-style control plane, a PaperTrail review interface, or the Yin et al.\ observability
profile can consume SwarmX records without sharing the underlying runtimes. Verification work such
as LEDGER's trace graphs therefore composes with SwarmX rather than competing with it.

Mokyr's useful-knowledge framework~\cite{mokyr2005} gives a broader rationale for joining
explanation and procedure. The framework distinguishes propositional knowledge about why a
technique works from prescriptive knowledge about how it is performed; it is one of the
influences drawn on in the scientific background for the 2025 Prize in Economic
Sciences~\cite{committee2025sustained}, where sustained progress is linked to lowering the
costs of finding, storing, transmitting, and verifying knowledge.
Applied to computational research, questions, hypotheses, claims, and evidence form the explanatory
record, while code, executions, configurations, workflows, and skills form the procedural record.
Their links allow a later researcher or agent to recover both the basis of a result and the process
that produced it.

We use these ideas with the FAIR principles as an institutional design lens for AI-assisted
science~\cite{mokyr2005,wilkinson2016}:
a durable knowledge substrate and commons boundary, interoperability infrastructure, epistemic
quality control, and governance and incentives. This four-layer organization is a synthesis used in
this article, not a taxonomy proposed by Mokyr. It separates the functions that software can supply
locally from the public archives, shared facilities, and incentive institutions on which cumulative
science also depends.

This article makes three contributions. First, it presents an architecture independent of a
particular client that connects native agent runtimes to append-only research records, content-addressed artifacts,
effect-fenced team tasks, sourced knowledge, and portable exports. Second, it describes the current
implementation through two executed workflows covering scientific records, multi-agent coordination,
and persistent knowledge. Third, it evaluates the software
with reproducible artifacts, integration tests, and bounded exhaustive checking of six coordination
invariants.

\subsection{Software architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} presents two orthogonal views. The institutional-function view is a
directional constraint: L1 preserves explanatory and procedural knowledge; L2 connects scientific
resources; L3 makes results inspectable and contestable; and L4 governs authority and scarce
resources. The current release provides a local substrate, evaluated adapters, process-level
quality controls, and workflow-scale governance. It does not itself constitute a public knowledge
commons or a funding, compute-allocation, credit, and reputation system; the dashed L4 boundary
marks this partial scope.

The operational-ownership view describes the implemented system: a client, native conversation
runtimes, SwarmX services, and user-controlled resources with portable outputs. A Host protocol
presents conversations and scientific resources to the client. DeepSeek Harness (DSH) Sessions and
Codex App Server Threads implement the same lifecycle, event, revision, branch, and approval
interface. Each runtime remains responsible for its history, agent loop, tools, authentication, and
storage; the adapter retains only the mapping to the native identifier and projects events into a
common client model. Institutional layers therefore add requirements without creating package
boundaries or transferring authority.

Science, Swarm, and Memory run below the conversation adapters. DSH
receives their aggregate tools through its native tool layer, while Codex receives the same operations through a Model
Context Protocol (MCP) carrier. The evaluated client packages these components as an Electron
application, but service ownership and records are independent of Electron renderer state. This
separation allows another interface to reuse the same runtime and service contracts.

\subsection{Software functionalities}
\label{sec:coordination}

% 中文：SwarmX 通过连接而非合并两类记录来实现有用知识框架。问题、假设、主张和证据构成解释链；Notebook 单元、实验运行、工件生成记录、任务效果和技能构成过程链。带 revision 的定位符、内容摘要和显式证据关系跨服务连接两者。这种结构同时保存结果如何产生以及结果为何进入科研记录。
SwarmX operationalizes the useful-knowledge framework by linking rather than merging two families
of records. Questions, hypotheses, claims, and evidence form the explanatory chain; notebook cells,
experiment runs, artifact-generation records, task effects, and skills form the procedural chain.
Revisioned locators, content digests, and explicit evidence relations connect the two across
services. This structure preserves both how a result was produced and why it entered the research
record.

% 中文：ScienceCore 为 Notebook、实验、图件、写作、科研记录、查询和导出提供紧凑工具接口，并可只读搜索本地 Zotero。只追加 SQLite Journal 记录科研实体及其关系，物化投影可由 Journal 确定性重建。工作区文件保持可变；文件注册为工件时经过类型核验、SHA-256 摘要和原子内容寻址发布。带 revision 的规范 sx 地址连接会话与这些对象，项目图可导出为 RO-Crate 1.3。图件还可以记录绘图代码摘要、脱敏运行环境和已核验输入来源。
ScienceCore provides a compact tool interface for notebooks, experiments, figures, writing,
research records, queries, and export, together with read-only search through the
\href{https://www.zotero.org/support/dev/web_api/v3/local_api}{Zotero local API}. An append-only
SQLite Journal records scientific entities and their relations, and materialized projections
rebuild deterministically from the Journal. Workspace files remain mutable; when a file is
registered as an artifact, it is type-checked, SHA-256-digested, and atomically published by
content address. Canonical, revisioned \texttt{sx:} addresses connect conversations to these
objects, and a project graph exports as RO-Crate 1.3. Figures can additionally record a
plotting-code digest, redacted execution environment, and verified input sources.

% 中文：Swarm 把多智能体工作表示为由读、写和知识任务组成的持久有向无环图。读任务可以并行，同一团队同时只有一个有效写入尝试，知识任务通过带证据的接纳完成。可能产生外部效果的工具调用先记录持久意图；结果不确定时暂停再次执行，直到操作者核验后置条件。任务提交后由另一成员或负责人给出结构化 verdict，崩溃恢复撤销活动尝试而不重放潜在非幂等效果。这些规则把协调状态从模型提示词移入可检查的状态机。
Swarm represents multi-agent work as a durable directed acyclic graph of read, write, and knowledge
tasks. Read tasks may run concurrently, each team has at most one effective write attempt, and a
knowledge task completes through evidence-bearing admission. A tool call with external effects
first records a durable intent; an uncertain result pauses re-execution until an operator verifies
the postcondition. After submission, another member or the lead records a structured verdict,
while crash recovery revokes active attempts without replaying potentially non-idempotent effects.
These rules move coordination state from model prompts into an inspectable state machine.

% 中文：Memory 是以 OKF Markdown 概念文档实现的共享语义记忆。它跨会话保存科研知识，提供显式检索、修订与确定性校验；原生 Agent 仍拥有会话历史和上下文管理。结构与引用校验不等同于科学事实验证。
SwarmX provides shared semantic memory implemented as owner-readable Open Knowledge Format
0.2 Markdown concepts. The store persists curated research knowledge across sessions and exposes
explicit search, read, create, update, deprecate, and lint operations through the Host's Memory
tool. Global and workspace scopes bound access; native agents retain ownership of conversation
histories and context management.

Memory records support source references, lifecycle metadata, and SHA-256 revisions. Model writes
require single-use approval, and updates require the previously read revision. Deterministic
validation checks document structure, citations, and workspace-scoped Science references; its
diagnostics do not establish the truth of a scientific claim. Here, semantic memory describes
the role of this persistent knowledge store, rather than a new automatic learning or retrieval
algorithm.

\subsection{Execution environment and safeguards}
\label{sec:safeguards}

% 中文：SwarmX 服务在用户机器上运行，并把科研记录保存到工作区文件、SQLite Journal、内容寻址工件和 Markdown 中。大语言模型执行遵循所选 DSH 或 Codex 运行时及其供应商配置；Notebook、Zotero、Typst、Git 和 DVC 集成连接用户已经安装的本地工具，并在依赖不可用时报告明确状态。
SwarmX services run on the user's machine and persist research records in workspace files, SQLite
Journals, content-addressed artifacts, and Markdown. LLM execution follows the selected DSH or
Codex runtime and its provider configuration. Notebook, Zotero, Typst, Git, and DVC integrations
connect to tools already installed by the user and report an explicit unavailable state when a
dependency is absent.

% 中文：Electron 渲染器没有 preload 或 Node 集成，启用上下文隔离与沙箱，导航限制在回环源，浏览器权限默认拒绝。宿主从当前原生会话派生工作区权限，并拒绝绝对路径、目录遍历、符号链接逃逸、跨工作区标识和过期 revision。Swarm 效果规则覆盖通过其工具载体执行的操作；运行时原生工具继续由相应运行时的沙箱和批准机制控制。
The Electron renderer has no preload or Node integration, runs with context isolation and
sandboxing, limits navigation to the loopback origin, and denies browser permissions by default.
The Host derives workspace authority from the current native conversation and rejects absolute
paths, directory traversal, symlink escape, cross-workspace identifiers, and stale revisions.
Swarm effect rules cover operations performed through its tool carrier; runtime-native tools
remain governed by the corresponding runtime's sandbox and approval mechanisms.

\subsection{Verified Swarm workflow and knowledge admission}

The second example exercised the Swarm and Memory services through a fresh Codex-backed client session.
Its input is a six-row synthetic germination fixture with three control replicates averaging 79\%
and three primed replicates averaging 87\%. Four members---a lead, arithmetic reviewer, provenance
auditor, and independent verifier---executed the dependency graph in Table~\ref{tab:swarm-example}.
The two read tasks ran independently. Their completion released a write task with authority limited
to \texttt{results/}; that task ran the declared summarizer and produced exactly
\texttt{summary.json} and \texttt{summary.md}. The verifier checked the arithmetic, source coverage,
output set, digests, and synthetic-data boundary before the lead admitted the result through the
dependent knowledge task.

\subsection{Bounded transition-system analysis}

The executable model abstracts Swarm's production transition system into a finite state space. A
state records current and authorized attempts, effect ownership and resolution, uncertain external
effects, knowledge admissions and facts, message delivery, coordination writes, and crash status.
Transitions assign attempts, begin and resolve effects, crash and recover, admit and write
knowledge, and deliver messages. The checker breadth-first enumerates this abstraction to depth 7
and deduplicates serialized states.

The guarded relation encodes the six coordination rules listed in Table~\ref{tab:fault-model}; the
paired unguarded relation intentionally admits successors excluded by those rules. At depth 7, the
guarded relation reached 1,030 modeled states without counterexamples, whereas the unguarded relation
reached 10,701 with counterexamples to all six invariants. The latter is a fault-injection sanity
check, not an empirical baseline; both results apply only to the stated abstraction and bound. The
exact JSON report is executable with \texttt{pnpm paper:model} and fixed by a regression test.

\section{Impact}
\label{sec:impact}

Version~0.1.0 has no third-party user at submission, so the impact discussion below describes
enabled research questions rather than demonstrated adoption.

By making ownership, revision, and admission explicit, SwarmX enables research on how heterogeneous
agent runtimes preserve scientific rationale, how effect recovery changes workflow reliability, and
how independently verified outcomes can be reused without copying private native transcripts.

Mokyr's framework~\cite{mokyr2005} maps onto the knowledge costs addressed by SwarmX. Project
queries and canonical locators support finding records; bounded reads and portable formats support
acquisition and transmission; revisions, content digests, verdicts, and evidence relations support
verification. Researchers can therefore follow a result across a conversation, computation,
registered artifact, claim, manuscript, and later knowledge entry. Markdown, content-addressed
files, and RO-Crate keep the durable record inspectable outside the client.

The institutional stack makes the corresponding division of labor explicit. SwarmX contributes
local components at L1, concrete interfaces at L2, and process controls at L3; roles, budgets,
approvals, and admission provide a limited workflow form of L4. Public repositories and preservation
services must make the L1 substrate discoverable and accessible, facilities must supply authorized
compute or instrument endpoints at L2, scientific communities must perform independent criticism
and reproduction at L3, and funders and institutions must provide the allocation and reward
mechanisms at L4. Portable SwarmX records can inform those institutions but do not replace them.

The architecture also gives research-software developers narrow integration points. A disciplinary
tool can retain ownership of its simulator, database, or instrument while registering selected
inputs and outputs with Science. A conversation runtime implements the Host adapter rather than
reimplementing the scientific services. A collaborative workflow can use Swarm's task, attempt,
effect, submission, verdict, and recovery boundaries. These separations allow user interfaces,
agent runtimes, and domain tools to evolve without migrating the research record into a single
application database.

For agent-assisted projects, the practical effect is continuity. Work can resume after a client or
agent process exits because task and admission state is journaled; a reviewer can inspect the exact
artifact and evidence revision behind a claim; and verified outcomes can become sourced knowledge
without copying private native transcripts into the publication package. The two examples show
these paths through the current implementation.

\subsection{Scope and limitations}
\label{sec:scope-limitations}

Version 0.1.0 supports single-user operation with DSH and Codex adapters. The evaluated interface is the
Electron client; an independent CLI client has not yet been evaluated against the same contracts.
Notebook, literature, paper, and versioned-data functions use separately installed JupyMCP, Zotero,
Typst, Git, or DVC environments. Native Codex and DVC boundaries were exercised directly, whereas
JupyMCP and Zotero were tested with controlled peers.

The owner-held substrate is local and portable rather than a public scientific commons, and its
default export policy does not imply publication, open licensing, or archival preservation.
Likewise, provenance establishes lineage rather than scientific validity, while Swarm roles and
budgets govern one workflow rather than institutional funding, compute allocation, credit, or
reputation. Claims about those higher-level outcomes require separate infrastructure and evaluation.

The examples use fixed synthetic data to isolate orchestration and provenance behavior; broader
evaluation should examine disciplinary workflows, independent users, and long-term adoption. The
bounded model explores two symbolic attempts and abstract external effects through depth 7, so its
result applies to the stated finite transition system. Additional clients and runtime adapters
should likewise be evaluated against the same ownership and recovery contracts.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

SwarmX connects native agent conversations, scientific computation, durable team coordination,
artifacts, manuscripts, and shared semantic memory through explicit ownership and revisioned links.
The implementation provides append-only Science records, content-addressed artifacts, effect-fenced
Swarm tasks, evidence-bearing memory admission, and portable Markdown and RO-Crate outputs. A Science
workflow and a four-member Swarm-to-Memory workflow were executed through the current client and
published with inspectable records. Reproduction commands verify the exported artifacts; automated
tests exercise core behavior and selected external-runtime boundaries; and a bounded model checks
six coordination invariants through depth 7. Within the four-layer institutional lens, this is a
local substrate, interface set, process-control system, and workflow-governance foundation---not a
substitute for the public commons and scientific institutions with which future deployments should
interoperate.
